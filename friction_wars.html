Friction Wars

A 30-minute exercise on why people do what is easiest at the moment of choice, and what that means for any change you ask of an organisation.

Facilitator notes
  1. The rule, 5 minutes. Show the two choices and ask the room to predict the fortnight with nothing changed.
  2. Design, 10 minutes. Each table gets the eight printed cards and 10 points. They choose cards and write down two predictions. Enter both here.
  3. The fortnight, 5 minutes. Play the 14 days. Pause on the days marked with a note.
  4. Debrief, 10 minutes. Show what each card did, then use the slider to show how little separates no change from complete change. Finish with the questions.

Print the cards and prediction sheets before the session. The card backs are not printed; their effects are shown in the debrief.

    How the model works

    At each choice, the effort of an option is its starting effort, plus or minus the cards that act on it, minus the strength of the habit, minus the pull of the time of day, plus any event on that day. The person takes the option with the lower effort. There is no randomness, so every table faces the same fortnight and every difference between tables comes from the cards.

    Each time an option is chosen its habit grows, faster if it is rewarding. When it is not chosen, its habit fades a little. A second card on the same option counts for 72% of its face value and a third for 52%.

    The numbers are set for teaching. They are not measurements.
